### 1.1 The Distinction Between an Asset, a Marketplace, and an Economy
To understand why Panel Profits was constructed, one must first recognize the catastrophic conflation that plagues traditional financial technology: the belief that displaying an asset's price history is equivalent to modeling an economy.

An **asset** is an object possessing intrinsic, aesthetic, or legal properties that can be described, cataloged, and valued. A certified comic book slab sitting in an archival vault is an asset.

A **marketplace** is a mechanical communications channel where bids and asks are posted, matched, and cleared. An auction house or an online classifieds portal is a marketplace. It executes transactions; it does not understand consequences.

An **economy**, by contrast, is a self-sustaining, dynamic complex system composed of:
1. Heterogeneous participants operating with asymmetric information, conflicting incentives, and differing risk horizons.
2. Formal institutional entities (firms, syndicates, funds, clearinghouses) governed by capital constraints and regulatory mandates.
3. Multi-asset transmission channels where an event in one corner of the universe forces collateral and balance sheet adjustments in another.
4. Feedback mechanisms where market prices actively alter the underlying fundamental reality of the participants.
5. An irreversible, persistent temporal ledger wherein past actions constrain future possibilities.
